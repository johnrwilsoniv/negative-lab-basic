FB5The strip is guided only by two 3 mm ledges

The film's lower edge rests on the floor of a slot in each side wall. Between the walls nothing is under it. With the first or last frame of a strip in the window only one slot holds it, and the strip pivots on that slot's 3 mm floor.

Now

The problem

    5A · A sill: raise the gate body's floor to the film's edge

    Recommended

      The gate body is filled up to the film's lower edge (y 23, where the slot floors already are), from the reference plane back to the backer. The strip then rests on one flat floor from the outer face of one wall to the other, and an end frame cannot drop, however few slots it is in. The pressure plate and the wedge plate get 20 shorter and stand on the sill.

        5B · Longer, tighter slots

        Partial

          The slot is carried out through a boss on each side wall, to the edge of the gate front (which still closes it), so each guide is 12 long instead of 3. Only the gate body changes. But the strip still pivots, and to stay inside the 1 mm margin the clearance over the strip must drop to 0.1, where a strip that is slightly wide or a slot that prints slightly narrow will jam.

            FB6A strip can go the wrong side of the pressure plate

            Each side wall's slot is 3.2 along the axis, but the channel the film belongs in is only 0.6 of it. Behind the channel are the pressure plate's square edge (1.8) and then 0.8 that leads behind the plate, into the pads and ramps.

            Now

            The problem

              6A · Slot as deep as the channel, with a funnel and a lead-in

              Recommended

                At the wall's inner face the notch is only as deep as the channel (0.6), and it widens to 2.0 at the outer face. The lens side of the funnel stays the flat reference plane, so the strip goes in straight along the face it will lie on. A 0.6 × 45° lead-in on each end of the pressure plate's front edge means that a plate lying forward (it has no springs) is pushed back by the strip's tip instead of blocking it.

                  6B · Close the back of the slot, chamfer the plate's whole edge

                  Alternative

                    The slot keeps its wide mouth but stops at the pressure plate's back face, so nothing leads behind the plate. A 45° chamfer across the plate's whole 1.8 edge steers a strip that enters anywhere in the slot forward into the channel. The strip must then step sideways by up to 1.8 within about 2 mm of travel.

                      Doing 5A and 6A

                      What would change in the code, in the order to build it. Nothing here has been built yet: each step should be checked against the assembly at SLIDE and LOCK, and the overhang test, before the next.

                        How these interact with the other findings: FB2's clamp stack and FB4's contact on the neighbouring frames are along the axis, and neither fix changes them. 6A cuts FB7's film-slot opening from 113 to 21 mm² each. Under 5A the wedge plate settles onto the sill instead of the floor, the same 0.5 drop as before, so FB8 is unchanged.
